Return to the opening request. A useful brief depends on what arrives and what can be checked, not merely on the fluency of its prose. Ask the room to explain one difference between the email story and the clinical scenario: the email story is the presenter's actual account; the clinical exchange remains an illustration with additional semantic, identity and review requirements. Ask a second person whether MCP replaces DICOM or FHIR. It does not: tool invocation and clinical meaning are different jobs. Finally ask who should define the missing-unit behavior. Clinicians must help define it with implementers rather than merely consume whatever a product returns. The central judgment is whether information is reusable for the next authorized tool, not whether residents can label implementation stages.
