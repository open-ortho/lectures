0–7 · Useful delegation

Spend two minutes retrieving the earlier modules. M1 proposed computers as arguably the most-used tool in orthodontic practice, then showed the existing problems: sharing clinical data, retaining reusable clinical work when changing software, and collecting data for AI and research. Recall the photo of a radiograph on a screen, patients re-registering after a software change, and repeated data conversion. These are workarounds, not the benefits of sharing.

M2 introduced the basic building blocks for data in computers: databases, storage and interfaces. It followed clinical informatics actors and imaging workflows, then showed the clinic as a network of connected systems rather than one application. APIs are application programming interfaces through which software can communicate; files are another exchange route.

Bridge to the next slide: with those problems and building blocks in mind, what would you delegate before the next visit? Ask what learners would want ready when they open the chart, before discussing how a tool would obtain the necessary records.
