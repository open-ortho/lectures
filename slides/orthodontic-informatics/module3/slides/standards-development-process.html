39–49 · Shape and test exchange

  • Visit brief → testable requirement: name who receives the data, what it must show and what failure must be visible—not just “make a good summary.”
  • Measurement context: patient, visit, date, unit and source let a clinician check whether a number belongs to this clinical situation.
  • Uncertain match: the receiver must flag doubt instead of attaching an observation to a convenient-looking visit or treating an old note as current.
  • Clinical contribution: residents can identify missing information and harmful outcomes without writing an API or choosing a file format.
  • Developer contribution: implement exchange behavior with clinicians, standards experts and data-governance staff; clinical judgment does not pass to the software.
  • Audience addition: ask for a missing-data rule—for example, show “history not supplied” rather than infer a normal history—and its visible pass/fail result.
