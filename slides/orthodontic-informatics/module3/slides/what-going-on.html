From clinical need to tested exchange

39–49 · Shape and test exchange

  • Use case: doctors lead: who needs which information for what clinical task, such as a visit brief; developers advise on concrete detail.
  • Functional requirements: doctors lead testable clinical outcomes, such as showing visit, units and source; developers review whether detail is sufficient to build and test.
  • Standard gap analysis: standards experts lead comparison with existing rules; bypass amendment when a standard is sufficient.
  • Standard amendment: standards experts take a needed change through the standards body’s proposal, review and approval process—not a unilateral team edit.
  • Standard: standards experts identify the existing or amended shared rules; these are not a working software product.
  • Profile: standards experts select and constrain standard usage for a workflow; a separate profile is not mandatory in every ecosystem.
  • Doctor review, 3–6: interested, technically savvy doctors may check that standards/profile choices preserve clinical intent; not every doctor needs standards detail.
  • Implementations: developers lead building and configuring sending/receiving software using the chosen standard and applicable profile.
  • Connectathon: developers lead interoperability tests in a selected scope; issues return to profile and implementation for refinement and retesting.
  • Doctor review, 7–8: optional review checks clinical needs; this map does not prescribe a universal clinician approval gate.
  • Stable, tested exchange: a joint outcome for the tested scope, not another lead process and not universal certification.
  • Overlapping roles: standards authors may also be developers; the colors identify contributions, not disjoint professions.
  • Conceptual path: read 1–5 across, then 6–9 back; this teaching map is not a deployment protocol or a universal approval sequence.
