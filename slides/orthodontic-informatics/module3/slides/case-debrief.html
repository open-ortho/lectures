49–56 · Apply the argument

  • Missing context: expose absent dates/units and uncertain encounter (visit) links; an unresolved measurement must not become a silently completed fact.
  • Originals and sources: keep the original note/image and traceable links so a clinician can inspect what the draft used.
  • “Not supplied” ≠ “normal”: absent history is a gap in the received information, not evidence of a negative history.
  • Restricted identity: give the consuming tool independently configured service permissions; filtering dangerous tool operations complements, not replaces, those limits.
  • Denied-operation test: demonstrate that a forbidden request is rejected as well as showing a permitted retrieval working.
  • Draft then review: the clinician checks the result; writeback (saving to the chart) still requires separate authorization and correct patient/visit placement.
  • Missing-unit test: remove a unit → receiver flags it and preserves the source without guessing; this observable repair alone does not prove clinical correctness.
