Repair the exchange, not just the answer

  • Show missing dates, units and uncertain encounter matches.
  • Keep originals and source links; “not supplied” is not “normal.”
  • Use a tool-specific restricted identity; test denied operations.
  • Return a draft for review; permit writeback only separately.

Test: remove a unit → the receiver flags it, without guessing.

Compare the pairs' answers with these repairs rather than declaring one mandatory phrasing. A measurement without units should remain an unresolved item, not become a silently normalized number. An image without an encounter association needs a visible uncertainty and source reference. Missing history cannot support a negative history statement. The consuming tool needs its own restricted identity, independently configured from the human and from other systems; removal of dangerous tools complements those service permissions. Demonstrate a denied request as well as a successful retrieval during later testing. The result remains a draft until clinician review, and review alone does not configure permission to write. A useful acceptance test has an input condition and an observable result, such as removing a unit and checking the receiver's warning. None of these repairs alone proves the brief clinically correct.
